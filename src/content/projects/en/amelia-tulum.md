## The place

Amelia Tulum is born of respect for the climate, the topography and the vegetation of the place.

The building rises above the ground, like a house on stilts, to touch the earth as little as possible. Everything else followed from that decision: the form, the shade, the way the jungle passes underneath.

It is a house that barely touches the ground.

## Where it is

Amelia is in Tulum, in Quintana Roo, on a piece of jungle with the gentle slope of the limestone. It is not a development or a community: it is a house, in a quiet residential part of the municipality.

We include it among the six places because it best explains the other five. When we say we build with the jungle and not over it, Amelia is what we mean.

## How it is built

The house rests on piles. Between the ground and the floor of the house there is air, shade and jungle: the vegetation keeps growing underneath and the rainwater runs as it ran before.

Raising it had consequences you can see everywhere. Ventilation crosses beneath and cools the house without machines. The shadow the house casts protects what grows below. And from inside, you are at the height of the canopy, not the trunks.

The materials are those of the place and the climate: wood, limestone, surfaces that age well in humidity and sun. There is nothing in Amelia without a reason.

## How you live here

You live upstairs. Mornings come in with light filtered through leaves, and the midday heat stays below, among the piles. Afternoons are spent on the terrace, which is half the house.

Amelia is finished and lived in. It is not a proposal: it is a place where someone wakes every day with the birds.

Living calmly is the sentence that closes it. Here it needs no explaining.

### Why is it on stilts?
To touch the earth as little as possible. Raising the house lets the jungle keep growing underneath, lets the rainwater run as it did before, and lets ventilation cross beneath and cool the house without machines.

### How does it cope with Tulum's heat?
Cross-ventilation underneath and the shade of the canopy do most of the work. The midday heat stays among the piles and afternoons are spent on the terrace.

### What is it made of?
The materials of the place and the climate: wood, limestone and surfaces that age well in humidity and sun. Every material has a reason for being there.

### Can a similar house be built somewhere else?

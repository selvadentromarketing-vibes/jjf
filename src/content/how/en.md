## Can I buy if I am not Mexican?
Yes. In the coastal strip, where Tulum is, foreigners acquire through a bank trust called a fideicomiso: a Mexican bank is the formal titleholder and you are the beneficiary, with every right to use, rent, sell and bequeath. Inland in Yucatán, in Izamal or Mérida, you can buy directly or through a Mexican company. We explain which suits your case.

## What exactly is the fideicomiso and how long does it last?
A contract with an authorised trustee bank. It is set up for a long term and is renewable; it is inherited and can be assigned when you sell. There is a set-up cost and an annual bank fee. We share the current figures of the bank we work with when we talk.

## Who is the notary and what does the notary do?
In Mexico the notary is a lawyer with public faith, appointed by the State. The notary gives the sale its legal form, verifies title, calculates and withholds taxes and records the deed in the Public Registry. You choose the notary; we can recommend those who know our developments.

## How is my money protected during the transaction?
With clear contracts and, when you prefer, an escrow account administered by a third party. Payments go to the development's accounts, never to individuals. You receive receipts and a payment schedule from day one.

## What costs are there beyond the price?
Closing costs usually include the notary's fees, the state acquisition tax, registry duties, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs permit and the constitution of the fideicomiso where it applies. We give you a written estimate before you sign anything.

## What do I pay each year?
The municipal property tax, the annual fideicomiso fee if you hold one, and the development's maintenance fee. We show you the current amounts for each place.

## What taxes do I pay when I sell?
Income tax on the gain, with deductions and, in some cases, exemptions for residents. This is where a good tax adviser earns their keep: before you buy, not after.

## Do I need Mexican residency to buy?
No. You can buy with your passport and a tourist visa. Residency is a separate decision with its own benefits; we can put you in touch with the people who arrange it.

## Can I buy remotely?
Yes. The fideicomiso and the deed can be signed through a power of attorney, and visits can happen by video. Even so, almost all our buyers come once or twice before deciding, and we would rather you came.

## Which currency do I pay in?
We tell you per project and in writing before any reservation, together with the payment method and the schedule.

## What services does the lot or the house have?
Each development has its own water, power, drainage and connectivity. We show it to you on the master plan and, better, on site.

## What guarantee do I have that what is promised is delivered?
Contracts with dates and penalties, documented construction progress, and a track record you can visit: our completed developments are lived in.

## Is the land private property? Was it ever ejido?
Each development has its own legal history and we show it to you with documents — the deed, the registry background and, where it applies, the full-title resolution — before you sign anything. If something is not clear, nothing is signed.

## Which environmental permits does the project hold?
We show you each development's current permits — federal and municipal authorisations, including environmental impact where it applies — and which area is protected by contract. Building with the jungle begins with having the papers in order.

## How long does it all take?
A reservation is signed in days; a deed or a fideicomiso takes weeks, depending on the bank and the notary. We give you a realistic calendar at the start.

## And after I buy?
We are still here. Administration of the development, contacts for building or renting, and a person with a first and last name who answers.

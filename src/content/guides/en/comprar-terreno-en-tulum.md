## What kinds of land there are in Tulum

Tulum is not one place. There is land in the town, in the strip that joins the town to the beach, in the hotel zone along the coast, and in the jungle inland. Each is lived differently, costs differently and is regulated differently, and the first decision is which of the four you want.

The town is where Tulum's daily life is: the market, schools, workshops. The strip between town and beach — where Aldea Zama stands — is the one most thoroughly ordered by masterplan, with streets, pavements and services drawn before the houses. The hotel zone is the coast, with its beauty and its fragility. And the jungle inland — where Selvadentro stands — is where the silence, the shade and the cenotes are still intact.

A beachfront lot and a jungle lot are not expensive and cheap versions of the same thing. They are two ways of living. It is worth deciding that before you look at prices.

## What to check before you reserve

The first thing is the legal history of the land, not the rendering. Ask for the deed and the land's registry history, and ask directly whether the land was ejido and, if so, with what documents it was privatised. A clear answer with papers is a good sign; a long answer without papers is not.

The second thing is permits. A development in Tulum needs federal and municipal authorisations and, where it applies, environmental impact approval. Ask to see them and ask what is still pending. We show you ours per development, current, and tell you what comes next.

The third is the developer: what they have finished and whether the finished work is lived in. Visit something that already exists before buying something that will. If you are buying off plan, the full checklist is in our guide to [buying preconstruction](/en/guides/comprar-en-preventa-en-tulum/).

The fourth is services: water, power, drainage and connectivity. You see them on the masterplan and, better, on site. A lot without its services resolved is a lot with a hidden cost.

## How a foreigner buys in Tulum

Tulum lies within the coastal strip Mexican law reserves, and there foreign buyers acquire through a trust, [the fideicomiso](/en/guides/el-fideicomiso-explicado/), with an authorised Mexican bank. The bank is the formal titleholder and you are the beneficiary, with every right: to use, rent, sell and bequeath. It is a long-term, renewable contract that is inherited and assigned on sale.

You do not need Mexican residency to buy: a passport and a tourist visa are enough. Nor do you need to be in Mexico to sign; the trust and the deed can be signed by power of attorney. Even so, almost all our buyers come once or twice before deciding, and we would rather you came.

If you are Mexican, you buy directly before a notary, with no trust. The rest of the process is the same.

## Who does what

The notary is a lawyer with public authority appointed by the State. They give the sale its legal form, verify the title, calculate and withhold taxes and register the deed. You choose the notary; we can recommend the ones who know our developments.

The trustee bank, if you are a foreigner, sets up the trust and charges to open it plus an annual fee. The developer — us — delivers the land with its legal history in order, its permits current and the services promised, under contracts that carry dates.

And your tax adviser, at home and in Mexico, is the person best placed to tell you what suits your case. That is where good advice is worth most.

## What it costs beyond the price

There are closing costs: the notary's fees, the state property acquisition tax, registry fees, the permit from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the setting up of the trust where it applies. And there are annual costs: municipal property tax, the trust's annual fee if you have one, and the development's maintenance fee.

The figures change over time and with the bank. That is why we do not publish them here: we give you a written estimate, for your specific lot, before you sign anything. And we tell you per project which currency you pay in and on what schedule.

## How long it takes

A reservation is signed in days. A deed or a trust takes weeks, depending on the bank and the notary. We give you a realistic calendar at the start and tell you when anything moves.

## Buying to build

If you buy land to build your house, the development will have a set of rules: heights, setbacks, how much of the lot may be built on and how much vegetation must be kept. Read it before you buy; it is what protects the value of your lot and your neighbours'.

Then come the architect, the design and the municipal building permits. In our developments we know the architects who have already built there and we put you in touch. In Selvadentro, for instance, each house is designed for its clearing, with the large trees kept.

## Questions

### Can I buy land in Tulum as a foreigner?
Yes. Tulum is in the coastal strip reserved by Mexican law, so foreign buyers purchase through a trust with an authorised Mexican bank. You are the beneficiary, with the right to use, rent, sell and inherit. You do not need residency: a passport and a tourist visa are enough.

### What documents should I ask for before reserving a lot?
The deed and the land's registry history; if the land was ejido, the documents by which it was privatised; the development's current permits, including environmental impact approval where it applies; and the building rules. A serious developer shows them without being pressed.

### Is it better to buy on the beach or in the jungle?
They are two ways of living, not two prices for the same thing. The coast has the sea and its fragility; the jungle inland has silence, shade and cenotes. Decide first how you want to live in Tulum, then look at lots.

### How long does buying a lot in Tulum take?
The reservation is signed in days. The deed or the trust takes weeks, depending on the bank and the notary. We give you a realistic calendar at the start.

### What does it cost beyond the price of the lot?
Closing costs — notary, acquisition tax, registry, the Foreign Affairs permit and the trust where it applies — and annual costs: property tax, the trust fee and the development's maintenance. We give you the figures in writing for your case, because they change over time and with the bank.

### Can I buy without travelling to Mexico?
Yes. The trust and the deed can be signed by power of attorney and visits can be done by video. Even so, almost all our buyers come once or twice before deciding, and we would rather you came.

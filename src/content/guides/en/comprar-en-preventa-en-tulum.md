## What buying preconstruction means

Buying preconstruction means buying a lot or a house before the development is finished, sometimes before the work has started. You pay on a schedule across the construction rather than all at closing.

It is the most common way to buy new build in Tulum, and it carries one consequence worth saying plainly: what you are buying is not the model or the render. It is the developer's ability to turn that into a place, with the paperwork in order and within the time promised.

Which is why all the diligence in a preconstruction purchase is about the party selling, not only about the thing being sold. What to look at in the land itself is in our guide to [buying land in Tulum](/en/guides/comprar-terreno-en-tulum/).

## What to ask for before you reserve

**The land's legal history.** The deed and the registry record. Ask directly whether the land was ejido and, if it was, with what documents it was privatised. A clear answer with papers is a good sign; a long answer without papers is not.

**Current permits.** The development's federal and municipal authorisations, including the environmental impact permit where it applies, and how much land is protected by contract. Ask what is still outstanding and when it is expected. Building with the jungle starts with having the papers in order.

**The master plan with services.** Water, power, drainage and connectivity, and how they reach your lot. Land without services resolved is land with a hidden cost.

**The rulebook.** Heights, setbacks, how much of the lot may be built and how much vegetation must be kept. It is what protects the value of your lot and your neighbour's.

**The contract, with dates.** With the delivery date written into it and penalties if it is missed. A contract without a date is not a commitment.

We show all of that, development by development, before you sign anything. If something is not clear, nothing gets signed.

## How your money is protected

With clear contracts and, when you prefer, an escrow account administered by a third party.

And with one simple rule that holds for any development anywhere: payments go to the development's accounts, never to individuals. If anyone asks you to transfer to a personal account, the conversation ends there.

From day one you receive receipts and a payment schedule. We tell you, per project and in writing, the currency and the method of payment before any reservation.

## How to tell whether they will deliver

The hard question in a preconstruction purchase has a simple answer: look at what they have already finished and see whether people live in it.

Not a render of something finished. Something built, handed over, lived in, that you can walk through. Aldea Zama is a planned community that now works as a town, with commercial streets and neighbourhoods; Amelia Tulum is a house on stilts, already delivered. Both can be visited.

Ask too how long the developer has been in the region and who answers when something goes wrong. A developer who will still be there after delivery shows it in how they answer before the sale.

## What happens while it is being built

Documented construction progress, at whatever interval the contract sets. If you bought from a distance, that is what stands in for walking the site, and it should reach you without your having to ask.

If the calendar moves, the right thing is to be told when it moves, not when the delivery date arrives. That is the difference between a delay and a surprise.

## What happens at delivery

The deed is signed, or the [fideicomiso](/en/guides/el-fideicomiso-explicado/) if you are a foreign buyer, and it is recorded. The annual costs begin: the municipal property tax, the trust fee if you hold one, and the development's maintenance.

And then we are still there: development administration, contacts to build or to rent, and a named person who answers. Delivery is not the end of the relationship. In a development that grows in phases, the neighbour you get a few years from now depends on us continuing to do the same thing well.

## How long it takes

A reservation is signed in days. A deed or a trust takes weeks, depending on the bank and the notary. The construction takes what the contract says.

We give you a realistic calendar at the start. We would rather give a date that holds than a date that sounds good.

## Preconstruction and building your own house

If you buy a lot in preconstruction to build on later, there are two calendars: the development's, which delivers streets and services, and yours, which starts when you are able to build.

Ask from when construction may begin and under what rules. In our developments we know the architects who have already built there and we put you in touch. At Selvadentro, for instance, each house is designed for its own clearing, with the large trees kept.

## Questions

### Is it safe to buy preconstruction in Tulum?
It is when you can verify four things: the land's legal history with deed and registry record, the development's current permits, a contract with a delivery date and penalties, and finished work by the same developer that you can visit. If one is missing, it is worth waiting.

### How is my money protected during construction?
With clear contracts and, when you prefer, an escrow account administered by a third party. Payments always go to the development's accounts, never to individuals, and you receive receipts and a payment schedule from day one.

### What documents should I ask for before reserving?
The deed and registry record for the land; if it was ejido, the documents of its privatisation; current permits, including environmental impact where it applies; the master plan with services; the building rulebook; and the contract with dates and penalties.

### What guarantee do I have that what was promised is delivered?
Contracts with dates and penalties, documented construction progress, and a track record you can visit: our finished developments are lived in.

### Can I buy preconstruction without travelling to Mexico?
Yes. The deed and the trust can be signed through a power of attorney and viewings can be done over video. Even so, nearly all our buyers come once or twice before deciding, and we would rather you came.

### What happens after delivery?
The annual costs begin, property tax, the trust fee if you hold one, and maintenance, and the development's administration continues. We give you contacts to build or to rent and a named person who answers.
